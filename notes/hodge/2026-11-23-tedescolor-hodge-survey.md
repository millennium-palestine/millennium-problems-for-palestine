# Hodge conjecture: survey of partial results and Mathlib requirements

Status of this note: a survey, not a proof. Nothing here is machine-checked, and nothing here
solves the Hodge conjecture. The Clay Mathematics Institute alone decides any prize.

**Verification status of references.** In this session I could not run web searches (the
search tool was not permitted), and GitHub code search was rate-limited. Every reference below
is therefore from memory unless it is marked "Mathlib path checked". A reader should check each
citation before relying on it. Where I am not sure a reference is exact (year, venue, or
attribution), I say so.

## 1. The statement

Let `X` be a nonsingular projective variety over `ℂ` of complex dimension `n`. Write
`X^an` for the associated complex manifold (GAGA identifies the two categories, so
the analytic and algebraic points of view give the same projective varieties).

- Betti cohomology: `H^k(X, ℚ)`, and after tensoring with `ℂ`, the Hodge decomposition
  `H^k(X, ℂ) = ⊕_{a+b=k} H^{a,b}(X)`, with `\overline{H^{a,b}} = H^{b,a}`.
- Hodge classes of codimension `p`: `Hdg^p(X) := H^{2p}(X, ℚ) ∩ H^{p,p}(X)`, that is, the
  rational classes whose complexification lies in `H^{p,p}`.
- Algebraic classes: for each irreducible closed subvariety `Z ⊆ X` of codimension `p`,
  its cycle class `[Z] ∈ H^{2p}(X, ℚ)`. Let `Alg^p(X)` be the `ℚ`-span of these classes.

**Hodge conjecture (rational form).** For every such `X` and every `p`,
`Hdg^p(X) = Alg^p(X)`.

This is the form in Deligne's exposition for the Clay problem (P. Deligne, "The Hodge
conjecture", in *The Millennium Prize Problems*, Clay Math. Inst. / AMS, 2006; from memory,
not checked in this session). Two remarks:

- The rational statement is the open problem. The integral statement (with `ℤ`
  coefficients and `H^{2p}(X, ℤ)`) is false in general. The classical counterexamples
  go back to Atiyah and Hirzebruch (1962, "Vector bundles and homogeneous spaces", Proc.
  Symp. Pure Math. 3; from memory). Later counterexamples on threefolds are by Benoist and
  Ottem; I am not sure of their year or venue, so I do not cite them further. A proof
  of the integral version would not decide the rational one, and vice versa.
- `Alg^p(X) ⊆ Hdg^p(X)` is a theorem (the cycle class of an algebraic subvariety is of
  type `(p,p)`). So the conjecture is the inclusion `Hdg^p(X) ⊆ Alg^p(X)`.

## 2. Known partial results

Each item says what is known, and what I believe the reference is. Items marked
"[unverified]" should be checked before use.

| Case | Status | Reference (memory) |
|---|---|---|
| `p = 0`, `p = n` | Trivial. `Hdg^0 = ℚ` is spanned by `[X]`; the top degree is spanned by a point. | none needed |
| `p = 1`, any `X` | Known. This is the Lefschetz `(1,1)` theorem: `H^{1,1} ∩ H^2(X, ℤ)` is spanned by divisor classes, hence `Hdg^1(X) = Alg^1(X)` after tensoring with `ℚ`. | S. Lefschetz, *L'analysis situs et la géométrie algébrique*, 1924; Griffiths and Harris, *Principles of Algebraic Geometry*, Wiley, 1978; Voisin, *Hodge Theory and Complex Algebraic Geometry I*, CUP, 2002 |
| All surfaces (`n ≤ 2`) | Known. For `n = 2` the only nontrivial degree is `p = 1`, covered by Lefschetz `(1,1)`. | follows from the row above |
| Threefolds, `p = 2` (`H^4`, codimension-2 cycles, i.e. curves) | Open for general threefolds. I know of no proof for arbitrary threefolds. | none needed; a standard open case, see Voisin (2002) |
| Fermat hypersurfaces | Known in some degrees. | Shioda, "The Hodge conjecture for Fermat varieties", Math. Ann. 245 (1979) [unverified details; later extensions by Ran, unverified] |
| Abelian varieties | Known for some special classes (for example some complex-multiplication cases). Open in general. Weil classes show that the Hodge classes are not generated by divisors in general. | Weil, "Abelian varieties and the Hodge ring", Collected Papers III (1977) [unverified]; Ribet, "Hodge classes on certain types of abelian varieties", Amer. J. Math. (1976) [unverified] |

What I have not verified and therefore do not claim: the exact range of Shioda's and Ran's
results, the exact set of abelian varieties where the conjecture is known, and any result
for abelian threefolds or fourfolds. Any of these would need a fresh check before it goes into
a later note.

**Reference textbooks** (I am confident these exist; the exact editions are from memory):
Griffiths and Harris (1978); Voisin, *Hodge Theory and Complex Algebraic Geometry*, vols I and II,
Cambridge Studies in Advanced Mathematics 76 and 77 (2002, 2003); Hodge, *The Theory and
Applications of Harmonic Integrals* (1941).

## 3. What a faithful Lean statement needs

The statement in section 1 mentions five objects, and each needs a Lean counterpart:

1. Smooth projective varieties over `ℂ`.
2. The analytic space `X^an` (or another source of Betti cohomology), and the singular cohomology
   `H^k(X, ℚ)`.
3. The Hodge decomposition, or equivalently the Hodge filtration `F^p` together with its conjugate.
4. The fundamental (cycle) classes of subvarieties and the map `CH^p(X) → H^{2p}(X, ℚ)`.
5. The `ℚ`-structure, so that `Hdg^p(X)` is a subspace of `H^{2p}(X, ℚ)`.

### What the pinned Mathlib contains

The project pins Mathlib at revision `905b95818eb32af7874a58b427f50c1711a5e96c`
(`inputRev v4.32.2`, from `lean/lake-manifest.json`). I checked directory listings at that revision
through the GitHub API, without downloading Mathlib or running `lake`.

| Need | Found in Mathlib (pinned revision) | Status |

| Algebraic cycles | `Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/AlgebraicCycle/Basic.lean`: a cycle is a function `X → R` with locally finite support, with Stacks tags. | Mathlib path checked. This is not the Chow group. I saw no rational equivalence and no Chow ring in the listing. |

A code search for "Hodge" returned only the Hodge star on exterior powers, a p-adic Hodge theory file, and the Hodge index theorem entry in the list of formalized theorems (not in Mathlib). The "de Rham" search was rate-limited and gave no result. | Absent as far as I checked. The check is incomplete. |
| Hodge structures (`ℚ`-structure, weights, `(p,q)` types) | Not found. | Absent as far as I checked. |

**Conclusion.** The left-hand side (`Hdg^p`) needs the Hodge decomposition, and the right-hand
side (`Alg^p`) needs Chow groups and a cycle class map. Mathlib at this revision has neither,
and it has no analytification or de Rham cohomology. A faithful Lean statement of the Hodge
conjecture cannot be written with Mathlib alone today. Even the Lefschetz `(1,1)` case, which
is an easy-to-state special case, needs the same infrastructure. The formalization plan
(a separate note) addresses this.
